The Quiet Comeback: Why Gen Z Is Returning to Paper Books

Surrounded by endless notifications, a growing number of young people are choosing printed pages and offline spaces.

Read Full Story →
Nov 12, 2026 • 4 min read

Walk into any independent bookstore on a Saturday afternoon, and you will notice something unexpected. Alongside longtime readers, the aisles are filled with teenagers and university students browsing paperbacks and buying physical notebooks.

After growing up entirely online, many young people are experiencing quiet digital fatigue. Algorithms decide what we watch, and notifications fragment our attention every few minutes.

Analog objects offer the opposite: they have boundaries. Choosing offline tools is not about rejecting technology — it is about deciding when and how we want to be connected.
